# Section 2: Literature Review

Pathfinding in dynamic urban environments is a complex challenge that sits at the intersection of classical graph search theory, real-time systems, and dynamic obstacle avoidance. This review surveys recent literature to contextualize the contributions of this work, focusing on modern enhancements to A\*-based algorithms and strategies for handling dynamic obstacles in real-world navigation.

## 2.1. Enhancements to A\* and Heuristic Search

The A\* algorithm remains a foundational component of modern pathfinding, but its classic implementation faces significant performance challenges in large-scale or complex environments. A systematic literature review by Foead et al. [Foead et al., 2021] confirms that classic A\* is often inadequate for modern demands, suffering from performance degradation and high memory overhead on large maps. This has spurred extensive research into A\* variants. For instance, Multi-Heuristic A\* (MHA\*) addresses the issue of "heuristic depression regions" by running multiple, simultaneous searches guided by different heuristics (both admissible and inadmissible), which has been shown to achieve significant speedups over Weighted A\* in complex robotics domains [Aine et al., 2015]. Further work has focused on improving search efficiency by modifying the evaluation function itself. One approach involves using dynamic weights that adapt as the search progresses; for example, giving more weight to the heuristic early in the search and more weight to the actual cost as the goal is approached, which has been shown to reduce traversed nodes and search time significantly [Li et al., 2023]. Other methods improve efficiency by eliminating redundant computations. Instead of backtracking when an obstacle is hit, an optimized A\* can simply mark the node as impassable (cost = ∞) and select the next-best child node, a strategy that has demonstrated up to a 70% reduction in path-planning time in complex environments [Liu, Wang, & Xu, 2022].

## 2.2. Strategies for Dynamic Obstacle Avoidance

Handling dynamic obstacles is a primary challenge in real-time navigation. A common theme in the literature is the use of cost penalties or potential fields to represent obstacles. The Artificial Potential Field (APF) method, where the agent is guided by attractive forces from the goal and repulsive forces from obstacles, is a classic approach. However, it is prone to local minima. Recent research has focused on hybrid methods that combine a global planner like A\* with a local, reactive method like APF or the Dynamic Window Approach (DWA). In such a hybrid architecture, A\* generates a global path, which is then used to guide a local planner that can react to immediate, unforeseen obstacles [Zhang et al., 2025; Liu, Wang, & Xu, 2022]. This decouples the strategic global plan from the tactical, real-time avoidance maneuver.

The concept of treating obstacles as sources of "cost" or "risk" is pervasive. In vehicular ad-hoc networks (VANETs), traffic density is often used as a direct proxy for obstacle-aware penalties, with routing decisions based on a dynamic score combining density and distance [Jerbi et al., 2006, as cited in your analysis]. A similar approach models traffic light delays by translating waiting times into an equivalent distance cost, which is then added to the A\* heuristic function, successfully guiding the search toward routes with fewer interruptions [Hu, Yang, & Huang, 2016]. More explicit penalty functions have been used in real-time navigation systems, where a large penalty factor is added to the utility function for any road segment reported as blocked by crowd-sourced data, effectively making the path prohibitively expensive [Wan, Ghazzai, & Massoud, 2019]. These "soft penalty" methods stand in contrast to our work's focus on a "hard exclusion" strategy.

## 2.3. Real-Time Routing in Dynamic Urban Networks

The specific challenges of urban networks, dominated by traffic congestion, have been extensively studied. A review by Isa et al. [Isa, Mohamed, & Yusoff, 2015] emphasizes the necessity of online, dynamic routing that can adapt to both recurrent (peak hour) and non-recurrent (accident) congestion. Many modern approaches treat traffic congestion itself as a dynamic obstacle. Self-adaptive evolutionary algorithms have been developed for Dynamic Vehicle Routing Problems (DVRP) that explicitly model congestion by adding a dynamic "traffic factor" to travel times between locations, allowing the algorithm to evolve solutions that are robust to changing traffic conditions [Sabar et al., 2018]. Another approach uses a multi-agent framework where individual vehicles play a "congestion game," learning from experience to select routes that maximize their personal utility, which is a function of driver preference, cost, and an "uncertainty value" for unexpected incidents [Yan, Hu, & Hu, 2018]. These works underscore the trend toward adaptive, data-driven routing that responds to real-time conditions. The use of real-time crowd-sourced data, as proposed by Wan et al. [Wan, Ghazzai, & Massoud, 2019], provides a direct data pipeline for such systems, enabling them to react instantly to user-reported blockages by re-calculating routes within a specified time window.

## 2.4. Synthesis and Research Gap

The literature confirms a clear and consistent trend toward more intelligent, adaptive, and context-aware pathfinding algorithms. It is well-established that classic A\* requires modification to be efficient in large, dynamic environments [Foead et al., 2021; Jern & Salomonsson, 2024], and a variety of methods exist for incorporating dynamic information, primarily through "soft" cost penalties within a heuristic or cost function [Hu, Yang, & Huang, 2016; Sabar et al., 2018; Wan, Ghazzai, & Massoud, 2019]. Hybrid architectures that combine a global planner (like A\*) with a local reactive layer (like DWA or APF) are also a common and effective pattern for handling dynamic obstacles [Zhang et al., 2025; Liu, Wang, & Xu, 2022].

However, a critical gap remains: the need for a real-time pathfinding algorithm that provides **verifiable safety guarantees** through a hard-constraint mechanism rather than soft penalties, while simultaneously retaining the search efficiency of a bidirectional approach. While many methods aim to avoid obstacles, they do not offer a provable guarantee that a path will not intersect a defined safety radius. This research addresses that specific gap by integrating a novel hard exclusion mechanism directly into a Bidirectional A\* framework, creating a solution designed to balance real-time performance with provable safety in complex urban networks.
