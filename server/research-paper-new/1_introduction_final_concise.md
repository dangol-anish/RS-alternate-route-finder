# Section 1: Introduction

Modern urban centers face pervasive traffic congestion, a critical issue that compromises economic productivity, environmental quality, and daily life. The challenge is particularly acute in rapidly developing cities like Kathmandu and Lalitpur, where burgeoning populations and vehicle ownership overwhelm existing infrastructure, leading to persistent delays and increased accident risks despite ongoing upgrades. Traditional navigation systems, relying on static or historical traffic data, are inherently ill-equipped to address the dynamic and unpredictable nature of urban environments—sudden accidents, road closures, or temporary hazards. This research tackles the pressing need for navigation solutions that can provide **real-time, verifiably safe, and efficient routes** amidst such dynamic obstacles, a critical gap where existing fast routing algorithms (e.g., Contraction Hierarchies) fail to adapt, and flexible heuristic methods often lack provable safety guarantees.

This paper introduces an **enhanced Bidirectional A\* algorithm** engineered for real-time urban navigation. Its core innovation is a **novel hard exclusion mechanism** that rigorously prunes any path violating a user-defined safety margin around dynamic obstacles, thereby offering verifiable safety guarantees absent in soft-penalty approaches. Coupled with efficient spatial indexing for rapid obstacle proximity queries, this algorithm is designed to effectively balance real-time performance, path efficiency, search reliability, and provable safety. Our contributions include the development and validation of this hard exclusion strategy, a comprehensive empirical analysis quantifying its multi-objective trade-offs, and empirical proof of its real-time feasibility on a complex urban road network. This work presents a robust framework for next-generation navigation systems capable of delivering adaptive, safe, and efficient mobility solutions for today's dynamic cities.
