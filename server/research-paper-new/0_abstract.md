Pathfinding in dynamic urban road networks presents a significant computational challenge, requiring algorithms that can navigate complex, unpredictable environments in real-time while ensuring safety. Existing high-performance routing algorithms often rely on static pre-computation, rendering them unsuitable for dynamic obstacles, whereas flexible heuristic-based methods frequently lack mechanisms for providing verifiable safety guarantees. This research addresses this gap by developing and evaluating an enhanced Bidirectional A\* algorithm that integrates a novel **hard exclusion mechanism**. This mechanism provides provable safety by strictly pruning any path from the search space that violates a user-defined safety radius around an obstacle, a direct contrast to common soft-penalty approaches. The algorithm's performance was rigorously evaluated through a full factorial experiment (1,600 runs) on a complex, real-world road network graph of Kathmandu and Lalitpur, analyzing the trade-offs between path safety, efficiency, and reliability across varying obstacle densities (ρ = 50 to 500) and four distinct safety radii. Our results quantitatively demonstrate the costs of safety, showing, for instance, that at ρ=150, widening the avoidance radius from 'standard' to 'wide' more than doubles the failure rate from 14% to 32% while increasing path length inflation. Critically, the algorithm consistently meets real-time performance thresholds (<100ms in most configurations) and exhibits a desirable "fail fast" characteristic in overly constrained scenarios. This work contributes a validated, high-performance pathfinding framework that successfully balances search efficiency with verifiable safety, offering a robust solution for next-generation autonomous and real-time navigation systems.
